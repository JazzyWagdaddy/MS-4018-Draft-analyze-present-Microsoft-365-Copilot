# 🧪 Lab: Your First 45 Minutes with Copilot in Microsoft Teams

> **The hook:** Imagine you had a coworker who never zones out in meetings, actually read the whole 200-message chat thread, and is happy to rewrite your grumpy email at 4:59 PM on a Friday. That's Copilot in Teams. Today you're going to put it to work.

| | |
|---|---|
| ⏱️ **Time** | 45 minutes |
| 🎯 **Level** | Beginner (new to Copilot) |
| 👥 **Format** | Pairs (Partner A + Partner B) |
| 🧰 **You need** | Microsoft Teams (desktop or web) + a **Microsoft 365 Copilot license** |

## Exercise 0 — Warm-up & Setup Check (4 min)

**🤔 Before you touch anything:** If you could hand *one* annoying part of your Teams day to an assistant, what would it be? Say it out loud to your partner.

**🛠️ Do it**
1. Open Microsoft Teams.
2. Look for the **Copilot icon** (the colorful ribbon logo). Check:
   - At the top of any chat or channel conversation
   - In the message compose box
   - In the Microsoft 365 Copilot app (in Teams' left rail or at m365.cloud.microsoft)
3. Pair up and decide who's **Partner A** and **Partner B**.

> ⚠️ **Don't see Copilot?** You may not have a license yet. Tell your instructor. You can still follow along with a partner who does.

> 💡 Microsoft moves buttons around a lot. If something isn't exactly where this lab says, look for the Copilot icon or the **⋯ More** menu nearby.

## Exercise 1 — The Grumpy Message Makeover ✍️ (7 min)

> **Story:** It's Friday. Your coworker just asked for the report *again*. You've typed something you definitely shouldn't send.

**🛠️ Do it**
1. Open a 1:1 chat with your partner.
2. In the compose box, type this (but **don't send it!**):
   ```
   I already sent you the report twice. Check your email. I'm not sending it again.
   ```
3. Select the **Copilot icon in the compose box** and choose **Rewrite**.
4. Try adjusting it:
   - Make it **more professional**
   - Make it **shorter**
   - Use a custom prompt: `Make this friendly and offer to resend the link`
5. Pick the version you like best and send it to your partner.

**Bonus:** Clear the box and ask Copilot to **draft from scratch**:
```
Write a short, upbeat message inviting my team to a 15-minute stand-up on Monday at 9 AM.
```

**🤔 Think about it:** Copilot gave you three versions. How did *you* decide which one was "right"? Who's responsible for what gets sent?

**🧩 4D tag:** **Description** (telling Copilot the tone you want) + **Diligence** (you own what you send)

## Exercise 2 — The 40-Message Mess 💬 (8 min)

> **Story:** You were on vacation for a week. Your team planned the office holiday party in a group chat without you. Now there are 40 messages and you have a meeting about it in 5 minutes.

**🛠️ Do it — Create the mess (3 min)**
1. **Partner A:** Start a group chat with your partner. Name it **🎉 Party Planning**.
2. Take turns posting these messages, one at a time (copy and paste is fine):

   | Who | Message |
   |---|---|
   | A | Okay, party planning time! Thinking Friday Dec 12th? |
   | B | Dec 12 works but a few people are out. What about the 19th? |
   | A | 19th is fine. Venue ideas? |
   | B | Rooftop place on Main St, or we do it in the office |
   | A | Rooftop is $40/head. Budget is $1,500 for 30 people |
   | B | That's $1,200, we're good. Let's book the rooftop |
   | A | I'll call them Monday to reserve |
   | B | I'll handle the ugly sweater contest prizes 🎄 |
   | A | Do we need a vegetarian option? Priya asked |
   | B | Yes, I'll ask the venue. Also need someone to send the invite |
   | A | Nobody volunteered for the invite yet lol |

**🛠️ Do it — Clean it up (5 min)**

3. **Partner B:** Open the chat and select the **Copilot icon** at the top of the conversation.
4. Try these prompts one at a time:
   ```
   Summarize this chat
   ```
   ```
   What decisions were made?
   ```
   ```
   List the action items and who owns each one
   ```
   ```
   What's still unresolved?
   ```
5. Compare what Copilot said to the actual messages. Did it get everything?

**🤔 Think about it:** Did Copilot notice that *nobody* volunteered to send the invite? Why does catching what's **missing** matter as much as summarizing what's there?

**🧩 4D tag:** **Discernment** (checking Copilot's summary against the source)

## Exercise 3 — The Late Arrival 🎙️ (10 min)

> **Story:** You got stuck in traffic and joined the meeting 5 minutes late. Everyone's mid-discussion. You don't want to say "Sorry, can someone catch me up?" (Again.)

**🛠️ Do it — Start the meeting (2 min)**
1. **Partner A:** Start a Teams meeting (**Meet now** works great) and invite Partner B.
2. **Partner A:** Turn on **transcription** (and recording, if your organization allows it).
   - Usually in **⋯ More → Record and transcribe → Start transcription**

> ⚠️ **This is the #1 "why isn't it working?!" moment.** Copilot needs the transcript to know what was said. No transcript = no meeting magic.

**🛠️ Do it — Role-play (4 min)**

3. **Partner B: Mute and stay quiet for the first 3 minutes.** (You're "stuck in traffic.")
4. **Partner A:** Read this out loud like you're running the meeting:

   > "Okay team, quick update on the website relaunch. We're pushing the launch date from March 1st to March 15th because design needs more time. Marcus is going to update the project timeline by Wednesday. Budget's still at $20,000, no changes there. One open question: do we keep the old blog or migrate it? I'm leaning toward migrating, but I want Jordan's opinion. Also, legal still hasn't reviewed the new privacy page. That's a blocker. I'll email them today."

**🛠️ Do it — Catch up (4 min)**

5. **Partner B:** "Arrive" (unmute) and open **Copilot** in the meeting controls.
6. Ask:
   ```
   What did I miss?
   ```
   ```
   What are the open questions?
   ```
   ```
   Is anything blocking progress?
   ```
7. **Partner A:** Ask your own question:
   ```
   What did I commit to doing?
   ```

**🤔 Think about it:** Copilot answered from the transcript. What would happen if someone made a decision in the meeting **chat** instead of out loud? Or mumbled it? What does that tell you about where Copilot "looks"?

**🧩 4D tag:** **Delegation** (letting Copilot do the catch-up) + **Discernment** (knowing its limits)

> ✋ **Partner A: End the meeting now** so the recap can generate.

## Exercise 4 — The Morning After 📋 (7 min)

> **Story:** It's the next morning. Your boss asks, "So what came out of yesterday's meeting?" You have 30 seconds.

**🛠️ Do it**
1. Go to the meeting you just ended (open its chat or find it in your **Calendar**).
2. Open the **Recap** tab.
   - ⏳ It may take a few minutes to show up. If it's not there yet, skip ahead to Exercise 5 and come back.
3. Explore what's there:
   - AI-generated **notes / summary**
   - **Follow-up tasks** (action items)
   - Transcript (and recording, if you recorded)
4. Ask Copilot about the meeting:
   ```
   Write a 3-bullet update about this meeting I can send to my manager
   ```
   ```
   Create a table of action items with owner and due date
   ```

**🔍 Accuracy check:** Open the transcript. Find one action item in the recap and confirm it's in the transcript. Is the **owner** right? Is the **date** right?

**🤔 Think about it:** You're about to forward this recap to your boss. What's the one thing you'd double-check before hitting send?

**🧩 4D tag:** **Diligence** (verifying before sharing)

## Exercise 5 — The Detective 🔎 (5 min)

> **Story:** Somebody said *something* about the party budget... sometime... somewhere. You don't remember if it was a chat, a meeting, or an email.

**🛠️ Do it**
1. Open **Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat** (the Copilot app in Teams, or m365.cloud.microsoft).
2. Make sure you're in **Work** mode (not Web), if you see the toggle.
3. Ask:
   ```
   What was decided about the holiday party budget?
   ```
   ```
   What's the new launch date for the website relaunch, and why did it change?
   ```
   ```
   What are my open action items from this week?
   ```
4. Look for **citations or references**. Click one. Does it take you to the original chat or meeting?

**🤔 Think about it:** Copilot just searched *your* stuff. Why is it a good thing that it can only see what **you** already have permission to see?

**🧩 4D tag:** **Description** (asking a clear question) + **Discernment** (checking the sources)
