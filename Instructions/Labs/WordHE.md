# Microsoft 365 Copilot in Word: Five Practical Hands-On Labs for Higher Education

A beginner-friendly, designed for **university faculty and staff**, and each exercise takes approximately **5–8 minutes**. All exercises use the same fictional university scenario so learners can focus on Word and prompting skills.

## What you will learn

By the end of this lab, you will be able to use Copilot in Word to:

1. **Draft a new document** from a short brief.
2. **Summarize a longer document** for a specific audience.
3. **Rewrite and improve text** for clarity, tone, and usefulness.
4. **Transform content** into a different document type.
5. **Review and refine a document** against defined requirements.

You will also practice a reusable prompting pattern:

- **Goal:** What do you want Copilot to do?
- **Context:** Why is the document needed, and who will read it?
- **Source:** What information should Copilot use?
- **Expectations:** What should the result look and sound like?
- **Follow-up:** What should Copilot check, correct, or transform next?

## Before you begin — 3 minutes

- Open Microsoft Word and create a blank document.
- Confirm that Copilot is available in Word under your university's license and settings.
- Keep this lab open in another browser tab.
- Use only the fictional content included below.
- Do not enter student records, personnel information, research data, confidential material, or other regulated information unless your university's policies allow it.

Copilot experiences may vary by license, application version, and university configuration. If a referenced option is unavailable, paste the supplied text into the document and use the Copilot experience available in your version of Word.

## The scenario

You work at **Lakeside University**, a fictional institution preparing to introduce **Focus Blocks**, a four-week pilot intended to give faculty and staff more uninterrupted time for course preparation, research, advising follow-up, assessment, and administrative work.

You will use the same scenario in all five exercises. Keep your work in one Word document so each exercise can build on the previous result.

## Fictional source material

Copy the following material into your Word document under the heading **Focus Blocks Planning Notes**. You will use it throughout the lab.

```
**FOCUS BLOCKS PLANNING NOTES**  
Pilot length: Four weeks  
Participants: faculty and staff in the College of Arts and Sciences and the Student Success division  
Working hours covered: Regular working hours in each participating unit  
Proposed focus period: Tuesdays and Thursdays, 9:30 a.m. to 11:00 a.m. local time  
During focus periods, participants should avoid scheduling nonessential internal meetings and pause non-urgent notifications when practical.  
Classes, scheduled student appointments, student support needs, campus operations, and urgent issues take priority.  
Department chairs and unit managers will collect short participant feedback at the end of weeks two and four.  
The pilot will be evaluated using participant feedback, the number of nonessential internal meetings scheduled during focus periods, and examples of teaching, research, student-support, or administrative work completed during uninterrupted time.  
University leaders want the announcement to be practical and positive, without suggesting that collaboration, meetings, or student communication are unhelpful.  
Open questions: Who will compile the pilot results? How will exceptions be documented? What date will the pilot begin?
```

## Exercise 1: Draft a new document — 5–7 minutes

### Your task

Use Copilot to draft a faculty-and-staff announcement introducing the Focus Blocks pilot.

### Step 1: Start with a basic prompt

Ask Copilot in Word:

```
Draft an announcement about the Focus Blocks pilot using the planning notes in this document.
```

Insert the response beneath a new heading called **Draft 1 — Faculty and Staff Announcement**.

### Step 2: Inspect the first draft

Review the draft and ask:

- Does it clearly explain what Focus Blocks is?
- Does it identify who is participating?
- Does it state when focus periods will occur?
- Does it explain that customer commitments and urgent issues take priority?
- Did Copilot invent a start date, owner, or policy that was not supplied?
- Is the tone appropriate for faculty and staff?

### Step 3: Strengthen the prompt

Run a more specific prompt:

```PlainText
Using only the Focus Blocks Planning Notes in this document, draft a faculty-and-staff announcement for the faculty and staff in the College of Arts and Sciences and the Student Success division.

Explain the purpose of the four-week pilot, the Tuesday and Thursday focus periods, the expected faculty and staff behavior, and the priority given to classes, student appointments, student-support needs, campus operations, and urgent issues.

Use a practical, positive tone. Organize the announcement with a short title, a two-paragraph explanation, and a bulleted "What faculty and staff should do" section. Keep it under 250 words. If a necessary detail is missing, write "To be confirmed" instead of inventing it.
```

Insert the result beneath **Draft 2 — Faculty and Staff Announcement**.

### Compare the drafts

Identify at least two ways the second prompt improved the result. Consider relevance, completeness, tone, structure, and unsupported assumptions.

### Checkpoint

You have completed this exercise when you have a usable announcement that is grounded in the planning notes and clearly distinguishes confirmed information from missing details.

**Skill unlocked:** Draft a structured document from a brief.

## Exercise 2: Summarize a longer document — 5–7 minutes

### Your task

Use Copilot to summarize the announcement and planning notes for a academic or administrative leader who wants only the information needed to decide whether the pilot is ready to launch.

### Step 1: Request a general summary

Ask Copilot:

```PlainText
Summarize this document.
```

Review the result. Notice which details Copilot prioritizes when the audience and purpose are not specified.

### Step 2: Request a decision-focused summary

Ask Copilot:

```PlainText
Summarize the Focus Blocks proposal for a academic or administrative leader deciding whether the pilot is ready to launch.

Keep the summary under 150 words. Include:
- The purpose and scope of the pilot
- The expected faculty and staff behavior
- How the pilot will be evaluated
- The unresolved decisions that could block launch

Do not add information that is not in the document.
```

Place the result under the heading **University Leadership Summary**.

### Step 3: Check the summary

Confirm that the summary:

- Preserves the four-week pilot length.
- Identifies the participating teams.
- Includes the Tuesday and Thursday focus periods.
- Describes the exception for classes, student needs, campus operations, and urgent issues.
- Includes the evaluation measures.
- Surfaces the three open questions.
- Does not introduce new facts.

### Mini-challenge

Ask Copilot to reduce the summary to **three bullets**, with one bullet each for **proposal**, **evidence plan**, and **decisions needed**. Compare what was lost or clarified through the extra compression.

### Checkpoint

You have completed this exercise when your summary is short enough for a leader to scan while retaining the facts needed to understand the proposal and make a decision.

**Skill unlocked:** Summarize a document for a defined audience and purpose.

## Exercise 3: Rewrite and improve text — 5–8 minutes

### Your task

Improve a poorly written paragraph without changing its meaning or inventing new requirements.

### Step 1: Add the rough paragraph

Copy this fictional paragraph into your document under **Rough Draft**:

> We are doing Focus Blocks because meetings and messages are a problem and faculty and staff need to get more work done. On Tuesday and Thursday mornings people should not bother each other or schedule meetings unless something important happens. Chairs and managers are going to check whether it works, and everyone needs to cooperate so the pilot is successful.

### Step 2: Ask for a clearer rewrite

Select the paragraph and ask Copilot to rewrite it:

```PlainText
Rewrite this paragraph to make it clear, professional, and constructive. Preserve the original meaning, but avoid implying that all meetings and messages are problems. Do not add facts that are not already stated here or elsewhere in the Focus Blocks Planning Notes.
```

### Step 3: Adapt the tone

Run a second rewrite request:

```PlainText
Rewrite the improved paragraph in a friendly, encouraging tone for faculty and staff. Use plain language, keep it between 70 and 100 words, and emphasize the benefit of uninterrupted work while acknowledging that customer commitments and urgent operational needs take priority.
```

Place the final version under **Improved Faculty and Staff Version**.

### Evaluate the rewrite

Compare the rough and improved versions. Check whether Copilot improved:

- **Clarity:** The purpose and behavior are easy to understand.
- **Tone:** The wording is constructive rather than critical or absolute.
- **Accuracy:** The rewrite remains consistent with the planning notes.
- **Precision:** Vague phrases such as “something important” are replaced with supported language.
- **Length:** The rewrite meets the requested range.

### Mini-challenge

Ask Copilot to produce a second version for department chairs and unit managers. It should explain the same idea in a more direct, operational tone and include the role department chairs and unit managers play in collecting feedback.

### Checkpoint

You have completed this exercise when the improved paragraph is clearer and more audience-appropriate without changing the underlying facts.

**Skill unlocked:** Rewrite text for clarity, tone, and audience.

## Exercise 4: Transform content for another purpose — 6–8 minutes

### Your task

Transform the faculty-and-staff announcement into a chair and unit manager implementation checklist.

### Step 1: Request the transformation

Ask Copilot:

```PlainText
Transform the Focus Blocks faculty-and-staff announcement and planning notes into a chair and unit manager implementation checklist.

Organize the checklist into:
- Before the pilot
- During the pilot
- End of week two
- End of week four
- After the pilot

Use action verbs. Include the responsible role when it is known. Mark unknown owners as "Owner needed" and unresolved details as "To be confirmed." Do not invent dates, systems, policies, or responsibilities.
```

Insert the response under **Chair and Unit Manager Implementation Checklist**.

### Step 2: Review the transformation

Check whether the checklist:

- Preserves the pilot's purpose and scope.
- Includes preparation for the focus periods.
- Accounts for classes, student needs, campus operations, and urgent exceptions.
- Includes feedback collection at the end of weeks two and four.
- Includes all three evaluation measures.
- Makes open questions visible rather than silently resolving them.
- Uses actions rather than repeating announcement language.

### Step 3: Improve usability

Use a follow-up prompt:

```PlainText
Revise the checklist into a table with these columns:
- Phase
- Action
- Responsible role
- Status or missing information

Keep each action concise. Do not remove any requirement from the existing checklist.
```

### Mini-challenge

Ask Copilot to turn the checklist into a short agenda for a 20-minute manager preparation meeting. Require a time allocation for each agenda item and label any unsupported timing assumption as a suggested allocation.

### Checkpoint

You have completed this exercise when the original content has been converted into an action-oriented format that serves a different audience and purpose while preserving the source requirements.

**Skill unlocked:** Transform existing content into a new document type.

## Exercise 5: Review and refine a document — 6–8 minutes

### Your task

Ask Copilot to evaluate the document against explicit quality criteria before making corrections.

### Step 1: Request a review, not a rewrite

Ask Copilot:

```PlainText
Review the faculty-and-staff announcement and chair and unit manager implementation checklist against every requirement in the Focus Blocks Planning Notes.

Create a review table with these columns:
- Requirement
- Covered, partially covered, or missing
- Where it appears
- Problem or risk
- Recommended correction

Also check for unsupported facts, inconsistent wording, unclear ownership, and missing open questions. Do not revise the document yet.
```

### Step 2: Inspect the findings

Verify that Copilot reviewed each source requirement individually, including:

- Four-week duration.
- Faculty and staff participants in both named university units.
- Tuesday and Thursday focus periods.
- Local-time treatment.
- Meeting and notification expectations.
- Customer and urgent operational exceptions.
- Week-two and week-four feedback.
- All three evaluation measures.
- Desired practical and positive tone.
- The three open questions.

If Copilot grouped several requirements together, ask it to separate them so each one receives its own status.

### Step 3: Correct only the weak areas

Ask Copilot:

```PlainText
Revise the faculty-and-staff announcement and chair and unit manager implementation checklist to correct every item marked partially covered or missing.

Preserve accurate content and the existing structures. Do not resolve open questions or add unsupported facts. After the revised content, add a section called "What changed" that lists each correction and the requirement it addresses.
```

### Step 4: Perform a final quality check

Review the revised content for:

- **Accuracy:** Facts match the planning notes.
- **Completeness:** Every requirement is addressed or visibly marked as unresolved.
- **Grounding:** Suggestions are distinguishable from source-supported facts.
- **Audience:** Faculty, staff, department chairs, and unit managers receive appropriately different wording.
- **Format:** The announcement and checklist retain their requested structures.
- **Safety:** The content is appropriate to use under your university's policies.

### Checkpoint

You have completed this exercise when Copilot has identified gaps before editing, corrected only the weak areas, and explained what changed.

**Skill unlocked:** Review a document systematically and refine it through follow-up prompts.

## Wrap-up — 2 minutes

You used Copilot in Word for five connected higher-education tasks:

| Use | What you practiced |
| --- | --- |
| Draft | Created a new announcement from planning notes |
| Summarize | Produced a decision-focused leadership summary |
| Rewrite | Improved clarity, tone, and audience fit |
| Transform | Converted an announcement into an implementation checklist |
| Review | Evaluated coverage and corrected documented gaps |

The most effective prompts in this lab combined a clear goal with relevant context, a defined source, explicit output expectations, and follow-up instructions. Prompt quality should be judged by the usefulness and accuracy of the result—not by prompt length alone.

Before using Copilot-generated content, always review it for accuracy, completeness, grounding, audience fit, formatting, and appropriateness for sharing.

## Reusable prompt template for Word

```PlainText
GOAL: [State the document action and desired outcome.]

CONTEXT: [Explain the purpose, audience, and situation.]

SOURCE: [Identify the text, document, or information Copilot should use.]

EXPECTATIONS: [Specify structure, tone, length, constraints, and quality criteria.]

GAPS: If required information is missing, [state how Copilot should label or handle it].

FOLLOW-UP: [State what Copilot should review, correct, compare, or transform next.]
```

## Optional take-home challenge

Choose a real, low-risk document from your work and repeat the five-part workflow:

1. Draft or extend content.
2. Summarize it for a decision-maker.
3. Rewrite one section for a different audience or tone.
4. Transform it into another useful format.
5. Review it against a checklist before using or sharing it.

Use non-sensitive content and follow your university's policies for Microsoft 365 Copilot, privacy, records management, research data, accessibility, and appropriate use.

## Facilitator notes

If you are leading this lab:

- Ask learners to keep all five exercises in one Word document so they experience iterative prompting.
- Encourage learners to compare outputs rather than search for one “correct” response.
- After each exercise, ask for one observation about what changed when the prompt became more specific.
- Reinforce that Copilot output can vary even when learners use the same prompt.
- Remind learners to verify content before relying on or sharing it.
- If a Copilot feature is unavailable, have learners paste the supplied content and use the available Copilot experience.
